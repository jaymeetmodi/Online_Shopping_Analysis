# A Statistical Study of B2C E-Commerce Behaviour in Vadodara City

A survey-based statistical study of online shopping behaviour among 323 respondents from Vadodara, covering platform preferences, payment methods, review habits and checkout barriers.

*Project report, Department of Statistics, Faculty of Science, The Maharaja Sayajirao University of Baroda (2025).*

---

## Overview

Platforms such as Amazon, Flipkart and Meesho have changed how people in Vadodara shop, but not everyone's experience is the same. This project collects primary data to understand who shops online, what influences their choices, and how demographic factors relate to shopping behaviour.

## Objectives

1. Understand the demographic profile of online shoppers in the target population.
2. Identify the leading e-commerce platforms, product preferences and drivers of consumer choice.
3. Examine the relationship between demographic factors and shopping preferences using the chi-square test.
4. Assess associations between demographic factors and shopping preferences using Goodman–Kruskal's test and categorical data analysis.
5. Carry out a SWOT analysis of the e-commerce landscape for Vadodara's population.

## Data and Sampling

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Type | Primary data (no suitable secondary dataset was available) |
| Instrument | 17-question questionnaire built around categorical and Likert-scale items |
| Responses | **323** |
| Target population | Residents aged 18 and above |
| Sampling | Convenience sampling combined with area sampling: the city map was divided into four quadrants of 25 grid blocks each, and five blocks were selected per quadrant |

**Respondent profile:** the largest age group is 18–24 (126 of 323), 170 respondents are graduates, students are the largest occupation group (90), and most respondents shop online occasionally.

## Methods

- Exploratory data analysis and descriptive statistics
- Chi-square test of independence
- Goodman–Kruskal gamma test for ordinal variables
- Partial odds ratios (contingency table analysis)
- Pearson standardized residuals
- SWOT analysis

## Key Findings

- **Platforms:** Amazon is the top choice across most product categories, especially electronics, home appliances and books.
- **Platform selection:** product quality and variety are the most important factors.
- **Research habits:** most respondents read reviews and ratings and compare options before buying. Quick visual feedback such as star ratings and user photos is most useful.
- **Payments:** UPI leads in adoption, with cash on delivery still a common fallback. Cards and EMI are used less.
- **Checkout barriers:** surprise costs are the biggest deterrent, followed by slow or inconvenient payment.
- **Chi-square tests (5% level):** age, educational qualification and income group are each independent of shopping frequency.
- **Goodman–Kruskal gamma:** gender shows no significant association with the importance of website design or the usefulness of product descriptions, and age shows none with review-reading habits. Income group has a significant, moderate association with monthly spending.
- **Pearson residuals:** businessmen, government employees and private employees lean towards frequent shopping, while homemakers, senior citizens and students lean towards occasional shopping. Males shop frequently more than females.
